**WHO I AM**

I am Onam Kumar Verma, a self-taught full stack developer from Delhi, India. I started out building interfaces. Then I got tired of shipping half a feature and waiting on someone else's API, so I learned to build that half too.

**WHAT I DO**

Three years later I work across the whole stack at Gyanis, an AI-powered EdTech platform serving students, teachers and coaches across six countries. I own the backend: the Node.js services, data models and payment flows behind the product. I still ship the React interfaces on top of them.

I got here the long way. React and Next.js first, then Node.js and NestJS, then Python, then the databases underneath all of it. That order turned out to matter. Coming from the frontend, I design APIs for the person who has to consume them: predictable shapes, honest error messages, no surprises in the payload. And having crossed over since, I can tell which "small" frontend request is actually a three-day schema change.

**WHAT I DID**

At Gyanis I built the credits and escrow ledger behind paid sessions, where funds are held on booking and released only on completion, with cancellation windows, refunds and coach payouts, designed for idempotency so no session double-charges. I designed the session lifecycle state machine that coordinates timers, notifications and wallet events, and built a unified notification service delivering events over Firebase Cloud Messaging, SendGrid and the WhatsApp Business API from one shared pipeline.

Before that I built a full-stack e-commerce platform with Next.js and FastAPI at GurukulVibe Bharat, freelanced on music streaming and job portal applications with Next.js, AWS Cognito and Strapi, and started out at Sahukar Consultancy building an e-commerce site and a B2B Makerspace PWA with React.js, Astro.js, Medusa.js, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS.

**MY LEARNING RESOURCES**

I have acquired web technology skills from various online resources, such as YouTube videos, freecodecamp, W3Schools, and official documentation etc. I'm still doing what I did in year one: picking up whatever the feature needs and learning it properly.
